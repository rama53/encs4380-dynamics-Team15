My system: Baymax drive motor

Team 15's Micromouse, Baymax, drives each wheel with a GA12-N20 encoder gear motor (6 V, 500 RPM) through a TB6612FNG driver from an ESP32-S3. Here we design a PI speed loop for one wheel: PWM duty in, wheel speed out. G(s) = K/(τs + 1)

1. The plant model and where its numbers came from

Input u is the PWM duty cycle in %, output y is the wheel speed in RPM. A DC motor's speed responds to a voltage step like a first-order system, so we model it as K/(τs + 1).

K, the gain (RPM per % PWM)
From the motor's rating: 500 RPM with no load at 6 V, which is 100% PWM on a 6 V supply. So K ≈ 500 / 100 = 5 RPM per %. Under the robot's weight the real value will be a little lower.
τ, the time constant (s)
The N20 sellers do not list a time constant, so 0.05 s is an estimate typical for motors this size. It must be replaced by the value from the step test below.

How we measure K and τ on Baymax (PWM step test):

  1. Lift the robot so the wheel spins freely, or run it on the floor for the loaded value.
  2. Hold PWM at 0, then step it to 50% and log the encoder speed every 5 ms for 1 s.
  3. K = final speed ÷ 50. τ = the time at which the speed reaches 63% of its final value.
  4. Move the sliders above to the measured K and τ. The gains and the code below update by themselves.

2. The spec and why it matters for Baymax

  • 0.6 ≤ ζ ≤ 0.8, the practical range from the lecture. A speed overshoot makes the wheel slip and the robot drift off the centre of the corridor, while a very damped loop is slow to reach speed on the speed run.
  • Zero steady-state error, so both wheels hold exactly the commanded speed. Any error between the two wheels makes Baymax curve instead of driving straight. A PI controller gives this for a step.
  • PWM within 0–100%. The driver cannot give more than full duty, so the design must not ask for it.

3. The final gains

For a PI controller on K/(τs + 1), the closed loop has ωn² = K·Ki/τ and 2ζωn = (1 + K·Kp)/τ. Solving for the gains:

Ki = ωn²·τ / K     Kp = (2ζωnτ − 1) / K
