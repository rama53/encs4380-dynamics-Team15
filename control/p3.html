Anti-windup, the Ts limit and reading the metrics
      
The controller calculates a trial integral and clamps the resulting command to the actuator range. With anti-windup on, it discards the trial integral if the command exceeds a limit and the error pushes it farther past that limit. With anti-windup off, the integral can build up during saturation and increase overshoot.

      
For P only on a first-order plant, the sampled loop has pole z = a − K·Kp·(1 − a) with a = e−Ts/τ. It leaves the unit circle when K·Kp > (1 + a)/(1 − a). With K = τ = 1 and Kp = 9, that happens at Ts ≈ 0.22 s.

      
Metrics describe the output y. Rise time uses 10–90% of r. Overshoot is measured against r. Settling requires y to stay within ±2% of r for the rest of the run. End-of-run error averages the error over the last 5% of the run. It estimates steady-state error once the output has reached equilibrium.
